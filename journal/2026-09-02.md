## The account bug, and what it was hiding

The app signed in anonymously on EVERY page load. 12,058 anonymous
accounts, ~1,000 a day. A CEO audit rolled up the real player docs: it is
not 13 humans, it is **seven, and five of them are 97% of every round ever
answered** — two of whom last played 27 August. Every funnel number this
product has ever produced had a denominator polluted by crawlers.

The leak is not retention. Of eight who answered a round, five came back.
Of ~18 who ever took a seat, **ten never answered anything**. The death is
between sitting down and the first question — which is the handle screen
the founder has now complained about twice.

## What the audits found that I had missed — including in my own fixes

**Soccer's Stats tab never worked.** I shipped per-sport deep cuts on the
1st and said soccer was covered. ESPN keys team stats by `name` AND
`label`; the client stored the label, cutsSoccer() asks by name, so all
four lookups were undefined. `host/snapshot.js` read the same feed by name
and was always right — two readers, one feed, and only the client wrong.

**Three of my new checks were theatre.**
  - `qa/first-tap.js` had `ok(rest.rail>0 || true, ...)` and printed "the
    rail rendered 0 tile(s)" as a PASS. The only unfailable assertion in
    128 suite files, and I wrote it yesterday.
  - `qa/deep-cuts.js` re-implemented the box parser inside page.evaluate
    and graded its own copy. Restoring the real CFB bug left it 19/19
    GREEN. When I added soccer to it this morning I reproduced the exact
    same disease inside the fix for the disease.
  - `qa/landing-wired.js` hard-coded five names, so the identical
    stranded-initialiser bug under a sixth name passed 25/25.

Both parsers are extracted and reachable now (`statPut`,
`parsePlayerBox`), the suite calls them, and every sabotage bites. The
allowlist is replaced by a generic reachability scan — four passes to get
from 92 false positives to zero, each exclusion a real JavaScript shape.

## The pattern, named properly

**1,428 of 1,818 catch blocks in the player app are `catch(_){}`.** So a
broken thing and a working thing with nothing to say produce byte-identical
output. That is why five features shipped dead and the only symptom was
"right now it looks the same". More tests do not fix this. Making an empty
result say WHY it is empty does.

## What I broke today

- **`git reset --hard HEAD~1`** on a tree with uncommitted work, to unpick
  a commit GitHub had rejected. It reverted the practice labels, five
  Spanish strings and a rebuilt schedule.json. Recovered in fifteen
  minutes; the gate's fingerprint caught it as VOID rather than shipping a
  mismatch. Check `git status` before a destructive command; use `revert`
  or a single-file checkout.
- A cap of THREE rounds for practice, which broke overtime — basketball
  has exactly four, so isLastRound(NR-2) became true. Four was right, and
  touches only baseball.
- A rail clearance of 52px that took a 320px phone's rail to 53% of the
  screen.
- Four sabotage attempts that "passed" because the sabotage file was a
  dotfile, then in the wrong directory. A sabotage that boot-fails is a
  broken experiment, not a passing check.

## The lesson

Yesterday's was "something existed and nothing ran it". Today's is one
layer up: **something was tested and the test could not have failed.** The
gate stops regressions. It does not notice absence, and it cannot notice
its own blindness. Two audits and a founder with a phone found ten things
120 suites were silent about.
